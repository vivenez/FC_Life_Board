Trusting God in the Worst of Times

DATE: 2026-06-28

MAIN MESSAGE

Two days after her seventeenth birthday in 1963, Laura (Welch) was
driving with a friend through Midland, Texas.

In a brief moment of distraction, she missed a stop sign.

Shockingly, the brutal crash that followed took the life of her
boyfriend, Michael (Douglas).

In an instant, everything changed.

She knew she was at fault and that reality stayed with her for years.

She later said no words could describe that worst day of her life.

For a long time, she struggled to move forward, but through it all, she
held onto one unshakable anchor: her faith in God.

Her faith in God sustained her through grief and guilt.

In time, she married, raised a family, and became a steady spiritual
influence in her home.

When her husband George later faced his own severe struggles with
alcohol, her faith helped guide him back to the Lord.

Together, George and Laura Bush point to their faith in God as the
turning point in their lives.

How about us? - What will we do when the worst day of our life comes?

For some, it will come as a sudden loss of life, of wealth, or
opportunity.

For others, it will be a devastating health diagnosis, a broken
relationship, or a personal failure that cannot be undone.

On that terrible day, will your great trouble drive you away from God,
or draw you closer to Him?

You'll need a strong and genuine faith to endure your worst day.

The early church faced such a day when Stephen, a man full of God’s
grace and power, was brutally killed for his faith.

His death marked the beginning of intense persecution against believers.

Acts 8 says: “Saul was one of the witnesses, and he agreed completely
with the killing of Stephen. A great wave of persecution began that day,
sweeping over the church in Jerusalem; and all the believers except the
apostles were scattered through the regions of Judea and Samaria. (Some
devout men came and buried Stephen with great mourning.) But Saul was
going everywhere to destroy the church. He went from house to house,
dragging out both men and women to throw them into prison. But the
believers who were scattered preached the Good News about Jesus wherever
they went. Philip, for example, went to the city of Samaria and told the
people there about the Messiah. Crowds listened intently to Philip
because they were eager to hear his message and see the miraculous signs
he did. Many evil spirits were cast out, screaming as they left their
victims. Many paralyzed and lame people were healed. So there was great
joy in that city.” (Acts 8:1–8)

This passage teaches us how to trust God when life falls apart.

First, remember who God is.

The worst day of your life does not change the character of God.

In our fallen world, suffering is real.

The Bible never promises a life free from pain.

But even on your darkest day, God is still good.

He is always faithful and still sovereign over all things.

Nothing that happens to you catches God by surprise.

He is the same God who sent His Son, Jesus, to die for your sins and
rise again.

His love for you does not waver when your circumstances change.

When everything else feels uncertain, the unchanging nature of God
becomes your solid foundation.

Second, remember what God can do.

From a human perspective, Stephen’s death looked like a devastating
defeat.

The church was scattered, believers were hunted and fear spread quickly.

But God was at work in unseen ways as 'believers who were scattered
preached the Good News about Jesus wherever they went.' (Acts 8:4)

What looked like disaster became a turning point for the spread of the
gospel.

Because of persecution, the message of Christ moved beyond Jerusalem
into Judea, Samaria, and eventually to the ends of the earth.

God used suffering to accomplish His greater purpose.

God brings good even out of what seems unbearably painful.

You may not immediately see God working in your and may not understand
it for a long time or even forever.

But God is always working for "we know that God causes everything to
work together for the good of those who love God and are called
according to his purpose.” (Romans 8:28)

That does not mean everything that happens is good but God is able to
take even the worst things and weave them into His good and perfect
plan.

Third, allow God to keep working through you.

Philip, a close associate of Stephen, must have been deeply grieved by
his friend’s death.

Yet Philip did not withdraw from his faith, but continued to serve the
Lord.

“Philip went to the city of Samaria and told the people there about the
Messiah.” (Acts 8:5)

Even in sorrow, Philip remained faithful and God used him in powerful
ways.

Lives were changed, people were healed, and Scripture tells us, “there
was great joy in (Samaria)” (Acts 8:8).

REFLECTION

When your worst day comes, you'll face a choice.

You can turn away from God, or you can cling to Him more tightly than
ever.

There is no middle ground that brings peace.

Continue to read your Bible, even when it feels difficult.

Continue to pray, even when words are hard to find.

Continue to gather with God’s people, even when your heart is heavy.

Simple acts of obedience help keep you spiritually connected.

Grief is real, and healing takes time.

But as you walk faithfully with God, He produces spiritual fruit in your
life like a deeper faith, stronger character, and even joy that cannot
be explained.

So when that terrible day comes in your life, please remember who God
is, remember what He can do and allow Him to keep working through you.

The same God who used persecution to spread the gospel can use your
suffering for His glory and for your ultimate good.

God IS faithful, even when life is not easy and we don't understand.
