WHO AM I?

MAIN MESSAGE

Today we sang about an important question: Who am I? Who am I beneath my responsibilities and my reputation? What gives my life meaning? Do I even matter?

The song “Who Am I?” is rooted in Psalm 8, where David begins not by looking inward, but upward as he writes: “O LORD, our Lord, your majestic name fills the earth! Your glory is higher than the heavens” (Psalm 8:1).

Before asking who we are, we must remember who God is. He is the Creator who set the moon and stars in place by His wisdom and power.

Then David asks, “When I look at the night sky and see the work of your fingers—the moon and the stars you set in place—what are mere mortals that you should think about them, human beings that you should care for them?” (Psalm 8:3–4). This is pure wonder.

David is amazed that the holy God who made the universe would care about weak, sinful, fleeting people like us. That wonder runs all through the question, “Who am I?” Why would the Lord of all the earth know my name, hear my prayers, and care about my life?

The Bible’s answer is not that we are important because of our achievements, appearance, possessions, nationality, position, or reputation. Our value begins with God. We were made in His image, known by Him, and created for His glory.

Our world constantly invites us to build an identity out of things that can change. A job may be lost. Health can fail. Children grow up. Other people’s opinions rise and fall. Success can disappear as quickly as it arrives. If these things become the foundation of who we are, our identity will always feel fragile.

But God’s knowledge of us is not fragile. Psalm 139 teaches that He knows when we sit down and when we rise. He knows our thoughts and all our ways. We are never hidden from Him.

Being fully known can sound frightening because God also sees our sins, failures, fears, and secret struggles. Peter knew that feeling after denying Jesus three times. Yet Jesus did not discard him. Christ sought him, restored him, and called him to follow again.

This is where the gospel answers our deepest identity question. We are sinners who need grace, but in Christ we can become forgiven, adopted, and loved children of God. Our failures are real, but they do not have to be the final word over our lives. Jesus’ cross and resurrection speak a better word.

Who am I? I am not the center of the universe, but I am known by the One who created it. I am small, but I am not forgotten. I am weak, but God’s grace is sufficient. I am sinful, but Christ offers forgiveness. I am temporary, but God calls me toward eternal life with Him.

That truth gives both humility and confidence. Humility, because everything good comes from God. Confidence, because our worth no longer depends on proving ourselves to everyone around us.

So when you feel invisible, remember that God sees you. When you feel insignificant, remember that Christ considered you worth seeking and saving. When your responsibilities become overwhelming, remember that your deepest identity is not merely what you do for others. When you fail, do not make your failure your name. Come back to Jesus.

Take a quiet moment and ask yourself: What have I been using to answer the question “Who am I?” Is my identity resting on something that can disappear? What would change this week if I truly believed that I am completely known and deeply loved by God?

Before we leave, let us look upward again. The God whose glory fills the heavens is mindful of us. The One who placed the stars also comes near to the brokenhearted. We matter, not because we have made ourselves great, but because the great God has chosen to know us, love us, and call us to Himself.

# PRAYER

Lord, our Lord, how majestic is Your name in all the earth. When we look at Your creation, we are amazed that You think about us and care for us. Forgive us for building our identity on success, approval, possessions, responsibilities, or anything else that cannot last. Help us remember that we are known by You and that, through Jesus Christ, we can be forgiven, restored, and welcomed as Your children. Give confidence to those who feel forgotten, grace to those burdened by failure, and humility to all of us. Teach us to live this week as people who belong to You. In Jesus’ name, amen.
