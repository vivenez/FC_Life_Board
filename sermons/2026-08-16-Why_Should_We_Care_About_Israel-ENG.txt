WHY SHOULD WE CARE ABOUT ISRAEL?

Sunday, August 16, 2026

# MAIN MESSAGE

Anti-Israel bias and outright hatred of Israel are plainly evident in the world today.

The United Nations repeatedly singles out Israel for far more resolutions than any other country, often ignoring severe abuses elsewhere.

For example, in 2024, the UN General Assembly adopted 17 resolutions against Israel, compared with only 6 against all other countries combined. That means 74% of all the resolutions for the whole world were against Israel!

And there has been a wave of anti-Israel protests across the US at universities like UCLA and Columbia. Anti-Israel activists have openly celebrated violence against Israel. Media and public discourse repeatedly demonize Israel while ignoring comparable or worse actions elsewhere. There has also been harassment, threats, and violence against Jews tied to anti-Israel sentiment.

Should any of this anti-Israel bias and hatred matter to Friendship Church?

Every Sunday, Friendship Church has the great privilege to gather as a global family of believers. We represent various nationalities, cultures, and languages, yet biblical principles are true for all of us. Despite our diverse backgrounds, we are united by one faith, one Savior, and one Holy Word—the Bible.

When we read Scripture, it is easy to focus on our personal relationship with God. But when we zoom out, we discover an extraordinary truth: God chose to use a small, humble nation with little worldly prestige to advance His global redemptive plan throughout history.

Notice how the Apostle Paul reflects on God’s divine sovereignty and choice:

“When a potter makes jars out of clay, doesn’t he have the right to use the same lump of clay to make one jar for decoration and another to throw garbage into? In the same way, even though God has the right to show his anger and his power, he is very patient with those on whom his anger falls, who are destined for destruction. He does this to make the riches of his glory shine even brighter on those to whom he shows mercy, who were prepared in advance for glory.” (Romans 9:21–23)

Out of the whole human family, God sovereignly chose Israel to be a special vessel through which His mercy and glory would shine to the world.

God’s connection with Israel began thousands of years ago when He called Abraham and made an everlasting covenant with him, promising him land, descendants, and blessing.

In Genesis, the Lord declared to Abraham:

“Leave your native country, your relatives, and your father’s family, and go to the land that I will show you. I will make you into a great nation. I will bless you and make you famous, and you will be a blessing to others. I will bless those who bless you and curse those who treat you with contempt. All the families on earth will be blessed through you.” (Genesis 12:1–3)

This covenant promise was never meant to stay contained within Abraham’s immediate family. Its reach was international, designed to extend through Israel as a nation and ultimately to every tribe, tongue, and nation across the globe.

Through Israel, God delivered two of the greatest gifts ever given to humanity: the Written Word and the Living Word.

First, God chose the Jewish people to preserve and proclaim His written revelation. Paul writes:

“Yes, there are great benefits! First of all, the Jews were entrusted with the whole revelation of God.” (Romans 3:2)

Every time we open our Bibles, we are reading Scriptures that were faithfully preserved and passed down through Jewish hands.

Second, and most importantly, it was through Israel that God sent the Messiah, Jesus Christ, as the Living Word. Paul reminds us:

“Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob are their ancestors, and Christ himself was an Israelite as far as his human nature is concerned. And he is God, the one who rules over everything and is worthy of eternal praise! Amen.” (Romans 9:5)

Jesus came as a descendant of Abraham to carry the sins of the world on His shoulders.

Why did God choose Israel?

Deuteronomy 7:7–9 reveals that God set His heart on Israel not because of their size or status, but simply because He loved them. God used Israel to proclaim His love and faithfulness to all nations.

Throughout history, God has shown faithfulness to His promises, and many who have stood with His people have seen His blessing in various ways.

On May 14, 1948, Israel re-emerged as a sovereign nation. Many believers see this as a remarkable event that resonates with ancient prophecies about God gathering His people back to their homeland.

Today, about 7.5 million Jewish people live in Israel, and Christians are watching how God’s purposes unfold as we await the return of Christ.

So, how should this truth shape our faith at Friendship Church today?

I want to challenge you spiritually in three specific ways today.

First, please consider your spiritual gratitude.

We must ask ourselves if we are truly grateful for our spiritual heritage. As non-Jewish believers grafted into God’s family, every spiritual blessing we enjoy—namely salvation, Jesus, and our Scriptures—came through Israel.

We cannot love the Savior without honoring the legacy of the people through whom He came. Let us cultivate genuine gratitude for the foundational roots of our faith.

Secondly, consider your prayer alignment.

Does your daily prayer life align with God’s priorities? Do our prayers focus entirely on our immediate personal needs? God calls us to align our hearts with His eternal promises.

Scripture says:

“Pray for peace in Jerusalem. May all who love this city prosper. O Jerusalem, may there be peace within your walls and prosperity in your palaces.” (Psalm 122:6–7)

As believers, let us commit to daily prayer for peace, justice, and safety in Israel and for all people in the region. And let’s pray that many more Jewish people will come to recognize Jesus as their true Messiah and receive eternal life.

Thirdly, are you living with prophetic awareness?

As believers, we should be living with active, expectant faith. God is faithful to His promises and has not abandoned His people. Israel continues to play a significant role in biblical history and in God’s unfolding purposes.

God’s promise of spiritual renewal among His people is a reminder that He keeps His word. Seeing God fulfill His ancient promises before our eyes should wake us up to the urgency of the hour.

There is no time to waste when it comes to sharing the gospel with all people while there is still time.

Although we are relatively few in number, may Friendship Church be a congregation that honors Israel and our spiritual heritage.

Amen.
