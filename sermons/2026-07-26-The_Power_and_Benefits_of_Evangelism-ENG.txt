The Power and Benefits of Evangelism

DATE: 2026-07-26

MAIN MESSAGE

Christians who have walked with the Lord for some time know that
evangelism is not an optional add-on to the Christian life, but the
natural outflow of a heart transformed by grace.

All believers are called and commanded in Scripture to share their
faith.

Peter tells us to “Always be prepared to give an answer” for the hope we
have, but to do it with gentleness and respect (1 Peter 3:15).

Yet in our culture, and sometimes even within the church, evangelism is
not viewed positively.

A Barna study of U.S. Christian millennials found that 47 percent
thought it was wrong to share one’s personal beliefs with someone of a
different faith.

Many people today view witnessing as unwise, unnecessary, or even
morally wrong.

Christians are often accused of being manipulative, intolerant, or
disrespectful.

Some even mock believers as if we think we are better than everyone
else.

Still, Scripture gives us truth that answers these cultural myths.

Through the Holy Spirit’s power, we can obey the Great Commission with
courage.

Evangelism brings many blessings, both to the hearer and to the one who
speaks.

First, verbal proclamation of God’s Word pulls back the curtains of
ignorance about our Creator.

In Acts 17:22-31, the apostle Paul stands before the people of Athens
and speaks to them about the “unknown God.”

Paul does not flatter their ignorance, nor does he avoid truth in order
to seem more acceptable.

Instead, Paul tells them that the God they do not know is the one who
made the world, gives life and breath, commands all people everywhere to
repent, and will judge the world through Jesus Christ, whom He raised
from the dead.

Some mocked Paul, but others believed.

Without bold proclamation, the curtains of ignorance remain drawn.

Paul asks, “How, then, can they call on the one they have not believed
in? And how can they believe in the one of whom they have not heard? And
how can they hear without someone preaching to them?” (Romans 10:14).

The first benefit of witnessing is that evangelism unveils who Jesus
really is, so the unreached can know their Creator.

Second, evangelism blesses hearers by igniting hope.

Every human being wrestles with the great questions of life like: Why am
I here? and What happens when I die?

The gospel answers those questions with living hope.

Jesus said to a grieving Martha, “I am the resurrection and the life.
Anyone who believes in me will live, even after dying” (John 11:25-26a).

Jesus' words shattered Martha's despair and gave hope in the face of
death.

When we proclaim Christ, God uses that message to awaken eternal purpose
and lasting hope.

Third, evangelism demolishes distorted views of reality.

The Bible teaches that people do not merely lack information; they
suppress the truth.

Romans 1:18-23 explains that people know God’s power and divine nature
from creation, yet refuse to honor Him, exchange truth for lies, and end
up with darkened minds.

Apart from Christ, people believe false realities where sin is good,
forgiveness is optional, and God is distant or indifferent.

Evangelism confronts those lies with the truth of God’s Word.

Fourth, evangelism opens the heart for faith to flourish.

It is the Holy Spirit, not our words, who saves.

Our role is to plant seeds that God uses to bring spiritual life.

Paul writes, “For God, who said, ‘Let there be light in the darkness,’
has made this light shine in our hearts so we could know the glory of
God that is seen in the face of Jesus Christ” (2 Corinthians 4:6).

Faith comes by hearing the Good News about Christ (Romans 10:17).

So when we speak the truth in love, God can use His Word to open blind
eyes and bring sinners to Himself.

Evangelism is not about our eloquence or personality.

It is obedience to a God who delights to use ordinary believers.
